Vegetables are the edible components of herbaceous plants that serve as a foundational element of human nutrition. Botanically distinct from fruits—which develop from flowers and contain seeds—vegetables encompass a diverse range of plant parts, including leaves, roots, tubers, stems, bulbs, and immature flower buds. Culturally and culinarily, they are defined by their savory, mild, or earthy profiles and are predominantly prepared as main courses, side dishes, or salads rather than sweet desserts. From a health perspective, fresh vegetables are exceptionally beneficial; they are compositionally high in water and dietary fiber while remaining notably low in calories, fat, and protein. Regular consumption provides a rich supply of vital micronutrients, including vitamins A, C, and K, essential minerals like potassium and iron, and powerful antioxidants that reinforce the immune system and protect against chronic diseases.
