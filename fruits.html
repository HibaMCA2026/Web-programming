Fruits are the mature, seed-bearing structures that develop from the ovary of a flowering plant after pollination and fertilization. While they are celebrated culinarily for their sweet, tart, and vibrant flavors, their primary biological purpose is to protect developing seeds and facilitate their dispersal throughout the ecosystem. Structurally, a fruit is defined by its pericarp—a protective wall divided into an outer skin (exocarp), a fleshy middle layer (mesocarp), and an inner layer (endocarp) enclosing the seeds. This scientific definition encompasses many savory foods traditionally categorized as vegetables, such as tomatoes, bell peppers, avocados, and cucumbers. Whether classified as simple fruits like apples, aggregate fruits like raspberries, or dry fruits like nuts and legumes, they serve as a vital dietary cornerstone for humans and wildlife, providing an essential source of hydration, dietary fiber, vitamin C, and potent antioxidants.
